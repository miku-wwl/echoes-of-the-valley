# 09 — Phase 5.2-B3: 2.5D art-direction pivot (illustrated caretaker, fixed camera)

2026-10-28 NZDT. Local branch `art/phase52-b3-2d5d`, started from the B2 review commit `aef87c09cd6ceee8ed183e59608c7b833268e481` (B2 is preserved untouched). **Gate B owner approval: PENDING. World expansion: NOT allowed. Human visual and gameplay QA: PENDING.** This document records what was built and measured; it is not an approval.

## Decision implemented

The owner abandoned the procedural 3D caretaker as the player's visual and approved a 2.5D presentation: the 3D valley, the controller, collisions, interactions, quests, crafting, saves and audio stay; the caretaker (and the three villagers) become illustrated sprites, under an owner-authorised fixed three-quarter camera.

## Where the art comes from (and what was not available)

No image-generation tool is available in this environment (no local Stable Diffusion/ComfyUI, no image API; Ollama only has a small text model). Drawing a character with code (circles, polygons) was explicitly ruled out by the brief, and was not done. The production sprites are therefore made **from the owner-supplied illustrations in `Echoes-Phase52-Original-Anime-Art-Pack.zip`** (OpenAI image generation, supplied by the owner; see `SOURCES_AND_RIGHTS.md` in the pack, copied to `tools/art/phase52b3/source/`):

| Supplied file | B3 use |
| --- | --- |
| `05-heroine-turnaround-reference.png` (front, side, back of the caretaker) | Cut from its parchment background with real alpha and colour un-mixing, then animated by 2D cut-out skinning: the source of **every caretaker sprite frame** |
| `01-heroine-portrait.png` | Dialogue bust (head-and-shoulders crop) and, unmodified, the satchel's Caretaker information |
| `02-npc-lineup.png` (Mira, Wren, Alder) | Villager idle sprites and dialogue busts |
| `03`, `04` | Not imported (concepts only) |

Every pixel of every sprite is resampled from these illustrations; no pixel is drawn, painted or generated by code. Where an accessory had to be removed (see E below) or a hidden piece of tights had to exist (a thigh moving out from under the skirt), the gap is filled by OpenCV inpainting from neighbouring pixels of the same illustration.

## Art pipeline (`tools/art/phase52b3/`)

1. `extract_views.py` — cuts the three turnaround views and the three villagers out of the parchment (GrabCut seeded by colour distance, soft edge alpha from the parchment distance, parchment colour un-mixed from edge pixels so no light halo remains).
2. `rig.py` — 2D linear-blend skinning of the illustration's pixels (bones with a hierarchy, smooth capsule weights, inverse warp by fixed-point iteration, cubic resampling). This is the Spine/Live2D principle, evaluated densely and offline.
3. `gait.py` — the side-view jog: the stance foot is planted by two-bone IK and travels backwards at a constant rate (the ground under a body moving at constant speed); heel strike, flat foot and push-off on the toe; the swing foot follows a Bezier arc. The cycle covers **2.85 m**, so at the unchanged controller speed (4.5 m/s) the game plays it at about 1.58 cycles/s (about 16 frames/s with 10 frames per cycle).
4. `hero_side.py` — side view as four layers: back body (coat tail, back skirt), far leg (the near leg's own pixels, darkened, half a cycle later), near leg (tights and boot, with the tights behind the hanging tassel painted in), front body. Bones for chest, head, arm, forearm, ponytail (3), scarf (2), coat (2), skirt, satchel, thigh, shin, foot.
5. `hero_frontback.py` — front and back views as one skinned layer each (the legs never cross there): the swinging leg shortens and lifts, hips sway, arms swing a little, hair, ribbon, scarf, coat tails and satchel follow with phase lag. Same gait timing as the side view.
6. `make_hero_sprites.py` — renders 10 run frames and 12 idle frames for **S, N, W and E**, at 0.5 x the turnaround's scale (about 289 texels per metre, 480 texels tall), packs one 4096 x 4096 atlas with colour bled into transparent texels (no dark or white fringe under filtering/mipmaps), and writes the frame rectangles, ground pivots, scale and stride.
7. `make_villager_sprites.py` — villager idle loops (breathing, head bob, slight sway) and the four dialogue busts.

### Direction count and mapping (four drawn directions, not eight)

* **S** (facing the camera), **N** (facing away), **W** (facing screen-left) come from the three turnaround views.
* **E** (facing screen-right) is the side view **without her left-side-only accessories** (garter, teal crystal pendant and red tassel), mirrored offline, with the run cycle shifted half a cycle so the near leg continues the far leg's stride when she turns. Without this, mirroring would have moved those accessories to her right side. The satchel, which hangs at her right hip towards the back, still appears behind her body in E (it should be in front of the coat there): that is a known limitation of mirroring one side illustration.
* **Diagonals:** facing within 35 degrees of straight away/towards the camera shows N/S; everything else (including all four diagonals at 45 and 135 degrees) shows the side views. A 6-degree hysteresis prevents flicker; a reversal passes through a side view for 0.07 s so it reads as a turn.

## Unity integration (isolated pilot scenes `Art/Phase52B3/Scenes`)

`Phase52B3Builder.Generate` copies the B2 pilot scenes (restored paths, canopy-fade foliage) and only changes presentation:

* `CharacterVisual2D` is a **visual-only child** of the unchanged `PlayerController` root (no collider, no rigidbody; one player, one save identity). `CharacterVisual2D5D` reads the root's movement and facing, advances the run cycle by distance / stride (a teleport is not running) and shows the frame. The B2 skinned hero and the original rigid hero stay in the scene, disabled, as recoverable legacy visuals (`-echoes-b3-legacy-hero` shows the B2 hero under the same camera for comparisons).
* `Echoes/Sprite25D` (own HLSL): the card is built in view space around the character's ground point, so the illustration always faces the camera at its true size whatever the pitch; every fragment takes the depth of the ground point pulled 0.45 m towards the camera, so scenery in front hides her, the ground behind her never cuts her, and grass at her feet does not slice the boots. Colour: lit by the scene's ambient and sun, darkened (to about half) inside real shadow-map shadows (canopies), fogged like the world. A second pass draws a faint silhouette where scenery more than 2 m in front hides her. `Echoes/ContactShadow` puts a soft shadow under her feet, aligned to the ground.
* Villagers: their 3D bodies are hidden (renderers only); `NpcVisual2D5D` sprites stand on the unchanged NPC objects, which keep their colliders, dialogue and idle logic.
* Dialogue: `DialoguePortraits25D` shows the caretaker's bust at the left of the dialogue box and the villager who speaks at the right; places and objects show only the caretaker. `DialogueBox` gained a read-only `Speaker` property (no behaviour change).
* Satchel: the unmodified `01` portrait remains the Caretaker information (label "Original AI illustration").

## Camera (owner-authorised constraint)

`FixedFollowCamera25D` (on the main camera, after the legacy orbit rig): **yaw 25 degrees (fixed), pitch 30 degrees (fixed), distance 8 m (mouse wheel 6-11 m), vertical field of view 35 degrees**, smooth follow with up to 0.8 m look-ahead, sphere cast against colliders (never closer than 3.5 m). No free orbit; WASD moves relative to the fixed view (W = up the screen). The pitch was chosen from Editor renders of four candidates (38/32/28/24 degrees): 30 shows building fronts and some horizon while keeping canopies and roofs mostly above the line of sight. The legacy orbit rig stays on the camera so the ending's slow drift (mirrored as a slow orbit around the player) and the scripted test cameras keep working. The controls hint now reads "WASD Move, Wheel Zoom, E Interact, I Satchel, Esc Pause".

## What still looks bad (author's honest list)

* **Front and back views keep the turnaround's A-pose arms** (held away from the body). Lowering them with the single-layer warp leaves ghost hands (tested at 8-20 degrees; rejected), so the idle looks like a reference pose, not a relaxed stance. A natural idle needs a drawn idle pose (new art), which is not available here.
* **Front/back running is subtle:** the legs alternate and lift, the body bobs and sways, hair and coat follow, but the arms barely swing and the upper body cannot twist. It reads as a light jog seen from the front or behind, not as a fully drawn run.
* **Side running** comes from one standing illustration: the far leg is a darkened copy of the near leg, the knee/boot bend is a warp of a straight leg, and a little tights texture shows where the skirt lifts. At game scale it reads as a jog; frame by frame it is not hand-drawn animation.
* **E (facing right)** is a mirrored illustration without her left-side accessories; the satchel still shows behind her instead of in front of her right hip, and faint inpainting smudges remain where the tassel was.
* **Only four drawn directions.** Diagonals show side views.
* **Style seams:** the caretaker and villagers are richly painted illustrations; the valley is still the simple low-poly/code-textured world (and Clover is still a low-poly 3D hen), so the characters are visibly more detailed than the world they stand in.
* **Close-ups are impossible in-world:** the sprites carry about 289 texels per metre, enough for the gameplay camera (the character is about 25-35 % of the screen height) but not for camera close-ups; the dialogue busts carry the face.
* **The source art is AI-generated** (supplied by the owner). Its turnaround is not geometrically exact (details differ slightly between views), which shows as small accessory differences when she turns.
